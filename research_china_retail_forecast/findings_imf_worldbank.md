# Research Findings: IMF & World Bank Forecasts for China Retail Sales / Private Consumption 2025-2026

## Summary

Both the IMF and World Bank project China's private consumption to remain subdued through 2025-2026, with growth decelerating from post-COVID recovery highs. Neither institution publishes a specific "社会消费品零售总额" (total retail sales of consumer goods) forecast line item, but both publish **private consumption** and **household consumption expenditure** growth forecasts, which serve as the closest proxies.

---

## 1. International Monetary Fund (IMF)

### Source A: 2025 Article IV Consultation — Staff Report
- **Publication Date:** January 28, 2026 (IMF Country Report No. 26/044)
- **Source URL:** https://www.imf.org/en/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/english/1chnea2026001-source-pdf.pdf
- **Chinese version:** https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/chinese/1chnca2026001.pdf

**Key Growth Forecasts (Table 1 — Selected Economic Indicators):**

| Indicator | 2024 | 2025 Est. | 2026 Proj. | 2027 Proj. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Real GDP Growth (%) | 5.0 | 5.0 | 4.5 | 4.0 |
| **Consumption (%)** | 3.9 | **4.6** | **4.0** | 4.1 |
| Fixed Investment (%) | 3.0 | 3.5 | 3.7 | 4.1 |
| Inflation (%) | 0.2 | 0.0 | 0.9 | 1.5 |

> **Note:** The IMF's "Consumption" line in Table 1 covers total household consumption (private + government), not specifically retail sales. The IMF does not separately forecast total retail sales of consumer goods (社零).

**Consumption Context from the Report:**
- The household savings rate remained elevated above pre-pandemic levels, weighing on consumption.
- The protracted property sector downturn and subdued consumer confidence continue to weigh on consumption.
- Real household consumption expenditure growth slowed to 2.6% in Q1 2025 — the lowest since Q4 2022.
- IMF projects total GDP growth at 5.0% in 2025 and 4.5% in 2026 (upgraded from October 2025 WEO by 0.2 and 0.3 percentage points, respectively).
- The IMF characterizes **transitioning to consumption-led growth as China's overarching policy priority**.

**IMF Policy Recommendations for Boosting Consumption:**
- Raising social spending (especially in rural areas) + accelerating Hukou reforms could boost consumption by up to **3 percentage points of GDP** in the medium term.
- Doubling social spending in rural areas could boost the private consumption-to-GDP ratio by **1.8 percentage points**.
- Comprehensive reforms could boost the consumption-to-GDP ratio by about **4 percentage points** over five years.

## 2. World Bank

### Source A: China Economic Update — December 2025
- **Publication Date:** December 2025
- **Report Theme:** Household Savings and Financial Intermediation
- **Source URL:** https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/600cd53e2bb24d516b8c3489e5d2c187-0070012025/original/CEU-December-2025-EN.pdf
- **Press Release:** https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/06/13/unlocking-consumption-to-sustain-growth-in-china-world-bank-economic-update

**Key Growth Forecasts (Table 1 — Selected Economic Indicators):**

| Indicator | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025f | 2026f | 2027f |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Real GDP Growth (%) | 3.1 | 5.4 | 5.0 | 4.9 | 4.4 | 4.2 |
| **Private Consumption (%)** | 1.7 | 9.0 | 5.2 | **5.1** | **4.9** | 4.8 |
| Government Consumption (%) | 5.3 | 7.3 | 1.0 | 4.5 | 4.3 | 4.2 |
| Gross Fixed Capital Formation (%) | 3.4 | 4.5 | 3.0 | 3.3 | 3.4 | 3.5 |

**Retail Sales Context from the Report:**
- The effect of consumer goods subsidies has moderated, with **retail sales growing at an average of 0.06% month-on-month (seasonally adjusted) over July-October 2025**, compared with 0.2% in Q2 2025.
- Household savings rate at 31% of disposable income in 2023 — significantly higher than the OECD average of 5.4%.
- Precautionary savings elevated due to gaps in social protection, income inequality, and property market uncertainty.
- World Bank June 2025 report ("Unlocking Consumption") projected 2025 GDP at 4.5% and 2026 at 4.0% — revised upward to 4.9% and 4.4% respectively in December 2025.

### Source B: China Economic Update — July 2026 (Rebalancing Growth)
- **Publication Date:** July 7, 2026
- **Report Theme:** Rebalancing Growth
- **Source URL:** https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/0cb2fc6dd88d4db3816dc0433b5cb49b-0070012026/original/CEU-July-2026-EN.pdf
- **Press Release:** https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/07/07/rebalancing-growth-china-economic-update

**Key Growth Forecasts (Table 1 — Selected Economic Indicators):**

| Indicator | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026f | 2027f | 2028f |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Real GDP Growth (%) | 5.4 | 5.0 | 5.0 | 4.4 | 4.3 | 4.2 |
| **Private Consumption (%)** | 9.0 | 5.2 | 4.7 | **4.5** | 4.7 | 5.0 |
| Government Consumption (%) | 7.3 | 1.0 | 4.7 | 4.5 | 4.6 | 4.7 |
| Gross Fixed Capital Formation (%) | 4.5 | 3.0 | 2.1 | 2.7 | 2.6 | 2.6 |

**Retail Sales & Consumption Context from the Report:**
- Real household consumption expenditure grew by only **2.6% in Q1 2026** — the lowest year-on-year growth since Q4 2022.
- Discretionary categories (transport, telecom, education, entertainment, household facilities) saw the sharpest slowdown.
- Essential categories (food, clothing, residence) held up relatively better.
- Precautionary savings rate at **32.4% in Q1 2026**, up from a pre-pandemic average of 29.6%.
- Policy support has provided a **smaller boost to retail sales in 2026**, with scaled-down consumer subsidies compared to 2025.
- CPI inflation averaged 1.0% y/y in January-May 2026, reflecting subdued domestic demand.
- World Bank July 2026 update revises 2026 private consumption growth to **4.5%** (down from the December 2025 forecast of 4.9%).

**Key Quote from World Bank Division Director Tatiana Rosito:**
> "Further strengthening the social safety net would be a key measure to boost consumption. Raising benefit levels, extending coverage to informal workers, and providing access based on residence could give households the confidence to spend more rather than save."

> **Note on terminology:** Neither IMF nor World Bank publishes a specific forecast for "社会消费品零售总额" (total retail sales of consumer goods). The closest proxy is **private consumption / household consumption expenditure**, which is broader (includes services, housing, healthcare, education) than retail sales statistics.

## 4. Key Factors Affecting Consumption Outlook

### Drag Factors (Subduing Consumption)
1. **Elevated precautionary savings rate** — 32.4% in Q1 2026 (vs. 29.6% pre-pandemic)
2. **Property sector downturn** — negative wealth effect, home prices down 11.9% (new) and 20.2% (secondhand) from 2021 peak
3. **Soft labor market** — youth unemployment at 17.7%
4. **Scaled-down consumer goods trade-in subsidies** in 2026 vs. 2025
5. **Deflationary pressures** — CPI near zero in 2025

### Support Factors
1. **Government subsidies** for childcare, elderly care, and preschool education
2. **Hukou reform** efforts to reduce rural-urban social protection gaps
3. **Retirement age increase** (2024 reform) expanding labor supply
4. **High-tech investment** driving some income growth
